# 14 · Gap analysis

Open questions with the trigger that reopens them. A decision closes a row: strike it
through and link the `D-`row.

| Id | Question | Why it matters | Options | Trigger | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G-001 | **SSO / external identity.** How do people authenticate once Requisit is not the issuer of their identity? | v1 issues signed personal tokens (D-005). A real customer authenticates against their own IdP; getting the seam wrong means rewriting every handler's notion of "who". | (a) OIDC authorization-code flow per organisation, mapping `sub` + issuer to a `person`; (b) SAML via a proxy; (c) accept an upstream gateway's signed header. The seam that keeps all three open: token → `OrgScope` happens in exactly one function (D-005). | The first evaluation that requires a customer directory, or any request to disable local tokens. | high |
| G-002 | **Multi-currency.** One currency per organisation, or per requisition, and what happens to a rule threshold when the currency differs? | Thresholds are amounts (D-008); comparing 5 000 JPY to a 1 000 EUR threshold requires a rate, and a rate requires a date, a source and a rounding rule — the moment money stops being exact (D-003). | (a) stay single-currency per org and reject foreign lines; (b) per-requisition currency with per-currency rule rows (no conversion — exact, ugly, honest); (c) conversion at submission with the rate stored on the requisition. (b) is the current leaning because it needs no rate source. | The first organisation with a second currency, or a catalogue item priced outside the org currency. | high |
| G-003 | **A PWA for approvers on the phone.** Does the approver queue need an installable, offline-capable app? | Approvals happen on phones between meetings. Server-rendered HTML (D-013) is fine to read but has no push, no offline queue, and posting from a flaky train is exactly the retry case D-010 covers. | (a) nothing — the mobile web page is enough; (b) a manifest + service worker on the same pages; (c) a separate PWA against the same API. | An approval-wait measurement (M-006) that shows the wait is the approver's device rather than their attention, or the first request for notifications (G-014). | medium |
| G-004 | **Postgres migration.** When does the SQLite file stop being enough, and what does the port cost? | D-002 is one file, one writer. Concurrency, a second instance, or a managed backup story all end at Postgres. The repository seam (D-004) is what makes the port bounded — if it erodes, the port stops being bounded. | (a) stay on SQLite with a replicated backup; (b) port the repository layer to `node:postgres`/`pg` and keep the same interfaces; (c) an abstraction layer from day one (explicitly rejected — it would be untested on the second engine anyway). | Sustained write contention (SQLITE_BUSY in production logs), a need for a second instance, or a customer requirement for managed Postgres. | medium |
| G-005 | **Multi-step approval chains.** Is one approval step enough, or do organisations need serial approvers (cost-centre owner *and* finance)? | D-008 deliberately ships one step. Real suites (Ariba, Intershop) model serial nodes. Adding a chain later changes the requisition state machine, the queue query and the audit shape. | (a) keep one step and raise the threshold; (b) an ordered `approval_steps` table per requisition, created at submission from the matched rules, each with its own audit line; (c) a full approval graph (rejected by the vision's non-goals). (b) is the shape to aim at — it is a superset of v1 with one step. | The first organisation whose written policy requires two signatures on one requisition. | medium |
| G-006 | **Webhook delivery for the order handoff.** Does the merchant side ever need to be pushed to instead of polling? | D-011 ships an outbox + poll. A webhook adds retries with backoff, a dead-letter path, a signing secret and an egress allow-list — a security surface the v1 does not have. | (a) polling only; (b) a webhook with HMAC signing, bounded retries and the outbox as the source of truth (the outbox stays either way); (c) a merchant-side SDK that polls for them. | A consumer that cannot poll, or a measured order-handoff latency (M-004) that the business calls too slow. | medium |
| G-007 | **Delegation and out-of-office.** What happens when the only person who may approve is on holiday? | Every B2B suite has it, and its absence is the failure people actually feel: a requisition stuck for two weeks. It touches authority, so it is the most dangerous convenience feature in the product. | (a) nothing — an admin edits the cost-centre owner (auditable, crude); (b) a dated `delegations` table `(from_person, to_person, from, until, scope)` consulted during rule resolution, with **both** names in the audit line; (c) an "approve on behalf of" admin action. (b) is the leaning; the audit rule from D-007 is non-negotiable in all of them. | The first requisition that misses a delivery date because its approver was away, or any customer policy that names a deputy. | high |
| G-008 | **Budgets and periodic spend limits.** Should authority depend on what has already been spent this period, not only on this requisition's total? | Intershop's budget rules and every real procurement policy are period-based ("50k per quarter per cost centre"). It changes rule matching from a pure function of the requisition to a function of history — and raises the question of what "spent" means (approved? ordered? invoiced?). | (a) per-requisition thresholds only (v1); (b) a `budgets` table plus a consumption query over approved requisitions in the period; (c) a running counter maintained in the approving transaction (fast, another truth to keep honest — see D-003 on computed totals). | The first customer policy stated as an amount per period rather than per order. | medium |
| G-009 | **The merchant-side agent.** Should the merchant get an agent that answers "why is this stuck?" and suggests reorders? | It is the second half of the brief's agent story and the part with the most commercial pull. It reads across requisitions, which makes its tenancy scope the whole question (D-004). | (a) nothing in Phase 1–2; (b) a read-only agent over one organisation's data with the same `OrgScope` as a person; (c) a merchant-scoped agent that legitimately sees several organisations — which would be the first cross-org read in the system and therefore always `risk:high`. | Arc 2 (the buyer drafting agent) shipped and measured, plus a merchant who asks. | low |
| G-010 | **Rate limits and abuse protection.** What stops a token from hammering the API, or from probing ids? | A single-container service with a synchronous database (D-002) is trivially stalled by a loop. D-016's uniform `not_found` closes the probing oracle, but not the load. | (a) nothing (trusted tokens, internal deployment); (b) a per-token token-bucket in memory; (c) a reverse proxy's limits in front. | Public exposure of the instance, the first untrusted integrator, or a measured p95 (M-002) that moves under a single client's load. | medium |
| G-011 | **Rule conditions beyond the amount.** Do rules need to match on commodity, supplier, cost centre or line attributes? | Ariba routes IT hardware to the IT manager; that is a condition on content, not on total. Adding it turns the ordered threshold list into a predicate list, which is the first step toward a rules language the vision forbids. | (a) amount only (v1); (b) add at most two optional equality columns (`cost_centre_id`, `category`) to the rule row, still first-match-wins; (c) a predicate DSL (rejected — unreviewable). | The first organisation whose policy names a product category or a supplier rather than an amount. | low |
| G-012 | **An HTTP framework.** Does the hand-written router (D-012) hold as the surface grows? | The trade in D-012 is dependencies versus schema validation, hooks and lifecycle. It flips at some size; guessing that size now would be inventing a number. | (a) keep `node:http`; (b) adopt Fastify with its JSON-schema validation; (c) keep `node:http` and add a tiny schema validator for bodies only. | The route table passes ~30 routes, or a second hand-rolled validation bug reaches a review. | low |
| G-013 | **Token revocation and rotation.** How is a leaked personal token killed before it expires? | D-005 tokens are self-contained; today the only answers are "wait for `exp`" or "rotate the org secret and reissue everyone". Roles are read live, so authority can be stripped — but the token still authenticates. | (a) short `exp` plus reissue; (b) a `revoked_tokens` / `token_version` column per person checked on every request (cheap — we already hit the database for roles); (c) full sessions. (b) is the leaning and is a small change. | Any real deployment, any token pasted into a chat, or the first customer security questionnaire. | high |
| G-014 | **Notifications.** Does an approver find out about a waiting requisition without looking? | The vision says no notifications in v1, which is honest only while the approver checks the queue. E-mail means an outbound dependency and a template surface; it also becomes the first place a requisition's amount leaves the system. | (a) nothing; (b) a digest e-mail via an SMTP relay, driven by the same outbox pattern as D-011; (c) push, which implies G-003. | The first measured approval wait (M-006) dominated by "nobody looked", or a customer requirement. | medium |
| G-015 | **Retention, export and deletion.** How long do requisitions and audit lines live, and what happens when an organisation leaves? | The audit is append-only by design (D-007), which collides with "delete my data". A B2B customer will ask for both an export and a deletion path. | (a) keep everything (v1); (b) per-org export (JSON/CSV) plus a documented retention period and a hard-delete of an organisation's rows as one transaction; (c) pseudonymisation of the actor while keeping the audit's shape. | The first offboarding, a legal review, or a database that outgrows its disk. | low |
| G-016 | **Linting without a TypeScript parser.** Does linting the emitted JavaScript (D-019) miss findings that a type-aware rule would have caught? | `npm run lint` builds `dist/` and lints that, because `typescript-eslint` would be a fourth devDependency and D-001 names three. The emitted JavaScript has no types left, so rules about `any`, floating promises, unsafe narrowing or unused type-only imports never run — `tsc --noEmit` covers some of that ground, but not all of it. *(#2 review, 2026-09-21: the coverage hole under this question is closed — `scripts/**` is emitted into `dist-lint/` and linted too, D-019 addendum. The type-aware question itself stays open.)* | (a) keep linting the emitted JavaScript; (b) add `typescript-eslint` and write a D-001 addendum for the fourth devDependency; (c) drop eslint entirely and rely on `tsc` strict plus review. | The first review finding that only a TypeScript-aware rule would have caught, or a second lint-shaped 🟡 in one arc. | low |
| G-017 | **A cost centre owned by its own buyer.** Who approves when the `cost_centre_owner` a rule resolves to *is* the buyer? | D-024 forbids a buyer deciding their own requisition, so such a requisition waits on a person who may never decide it. It is honest — nothing is silently approved — but it is stuck, and it is visible only as a `submitted` row with no decision line. | (a) fall through to `finance` when owner = buyer; (b) refuse at submission with a named reason; (c) the delegation of G-007. | The first requisition that waits on its own buyer: `awaiting.personId === buyerPersonId` on a `submitted` row. | medium |
| G-018 | **Rule rows and retroactivity.** May a rule row referenced by a requisition ever be edited in place? | Approval re-reads the stored `rule_id` (D-008 addendum, #3) and nothing snapshots `approver_kind`/`max_total_minor` onto the requisition. v1 has no rule-editing path — rows are seeded — so the question is dormant, not answered: an `UPDATE` of a referenced row would silently change who may approve an in-flight requisition. **Verified (#3 fix round, 2026-09-21):** not hypothetical — `UPDATE approval_rules SET approver_kind='finance'` on the row a submitted requisition stored refuses the resolved cost-centre owner with `403` and lets finance approve. Only an **INSERT** is neutralised by the stored `rule_id`; D-008's addendum, `rulesRepo.byId`'s comment and T2 of `docs/08-security.md` said "edit" and now say so correctly. | (a) replace-as-insert: a rule edit writes a new row with a new id and a new seq and never touches a referenced one; (b) snapshot `approver_kind` and `max_total_minor` onto the requisition at submission; (c) forbid editing a row any requisition references. | The first rule-editing endpoint or admin screen. | high |
| G-019 | **Within-organisation visibility.** Should every member of an organisation see every requisition of that organisation? | D-024 says yes, which is what the shared queue view implies. A customer who treats amounts as confidential between buyers, or between cost centres, needs a finer cut — and that cut changes the list query, the detail route and the audit history, not just a filter in the UI. | (a) keep organisation-wide read; (b) restrict to buyer + resolved approver + finance; (c) a per-cost-centre visibility rule. | The first customer policy that hides amounts or requisitions between buyers or cost centres. | medium |
| G-020 | **A real session, instead of the bearer token in a cookie.** The page session cookie carries the personal token itself (D-025). | Signing out clears this browser's copy and nothing else: the token stays valid until its `exp`, and a copy taken before the sign-out still works. That is the same residual as G-013, now with a second place the token can be taken from. | (a) keep it — the cookie is `HttpOnly` and the token already has an `exp`; (b) a server-side `sessions` table keyed by an opaque id, revocable at sign-out, which is also the natural home for G-013's revocation; (c) short-lived tokens plus a refresh at sign-in. (b) and G-013's option (b) are the same piece of work. | G-013 being answered, the first deployment outside the demo, or any sign-out that has to mean "that token is dead now". | high |
| G-021 | **A requisition-level note.** The draft editor's free text is the *line* `description`; there is no field for context that belongs to the whole requisition. | The issue that built the pages (#4) named a "note" in the editor, and the schema has no column for one — so the note is per line, which is honest but forces a buyer explaining *why* they are ordering to pick a line to say it on. It is also where a drafting agent's rationale would naturally go (Arc 2). | (a) leave it per line; (b) a `note` column on `requisitions` with the same 500-character bound and the same escaping, shown on the detail and in the editor; (c) a first line of the audit history written at draft creation carrying the note (no schema change — the audit already has `reason`). | The first buyer who writes the same sentence onto every line, or the drafting agent needing somewhere to put its reasoning. | low |
